Depth to parallel; bar travels in a straight vertical path. Knees track over the second toe throughout. Brace hard before the descent — hold that brace until you are back at the top.

The highest-return compound movement in the programme. It activates more total muscle mass simultaneously than almost any other exercise — quads, glutes, hamstrings, and core all working together. Single arm; load one end of a barbell into a landmine attachment. Push from shoulder height at a 45° arc to full extension. This pressing angle is mechanically safer for the shoulder than a straight overhead press — load it accordingly.

01 — Health Modifier
PCOS — what it means for your body and your training

PCOS is a hormonal condition — not a character flaw, not a life sentence, and certainly not a reason to train around yourself instead of for yourself.

Here is what it actually means: PCOS disrupts how your cells respond to insulin. When insulin sensitivity is low, your body stores energy differently — it accumulates fat centrally, and it resists the hormonal signals that would otherwise direct energy toward muscle growth. Combined with elevated androgens and a tendency toward elevated cortisol under stress, it can feel as though the body is working against you. It is not. It just requires a different sequence.

Resistance training is not a supplement to treatment — it is treatment. The 2023 International Evidence-Based PCOS Guidelines recommend vigorous resistance exercise as a first-line intervention. The RPE 8 sets in this programme produce the strongest improvements in insulin sensitivity, androgen levels, and reproductive hormones of any intervention studied. This matters. What you are doing in the gym is clinically significant.

If you have a regular natural cycle, your energy and recovery capacity will shift across the month. The first half tends to support higher loads and better recovery; the second half may call for maintained volume with slightly reduced peak intensity. Track how you feel week to week — the data from your own body is the most precise instrument available.

02 — Programme Design
Why this programme looks the way it does

An Upper/Lower split, four days a week, is not a generic choice. With your goals — muscle hypertrophy, fat loss, and PCOS metabolic management — this structure gives you the best return on the time you invest.

Morning sessions improve glucose disposal throughout the day. This matters specifically for you — training in the morning is associated with better metabolic outcomes for women with insulin resistance. It is not a preference; it is a priority.

Compound lifts first. Squats, Romanian deadlifts, hip thrusts, presses, and rows activate the largest muscle groups and drive the cellular changes that insulin-resistant muscle needs. Isolation work builds on this foundation — it does not replace it.

RPE over percentage maxes. RPE — rate of perceived exertion — calibrates to how you feel on any given day. On a hard week, stressful work, or late in your cycle, RPE 8 looks different to RPE 8 on a fresh day. The system adjusts itself. This is more intelligent than a fixed percentage, and it protects against overreaching when recovery is compromised.

Progressive loading via double progression: complete the top of the rep range with good form, then add load at the next session. The programme begins at 3 × 12 — a rep range that builds the technique, the motor patterns, and the connective tissue capacity needed before heavier loads are introduced. Each phase builds on the last. Nothing is arbitrary.

03 — Nutrition
Dietary principles

The meal plan is built around three priorities: supporting muscle growth, managing insulin, and reducing inflammation. These are not in conflict — the same food choices serve all three.

Protein target: 159–177 g per day (1.8–2.0 g/kg body weight). This is the range the research supports for muscle retention and growth alongside a caloric deficit. Distribute it across the day — not all at one meal. Every meal should contain a meaningful protein source.

Low glycaemic index foods are the default. Slow-release carbohydrates — sweet potato, oats, legumes, whole grains — improve insulin sensitivity over time. This is not about avoidance; it is about choosing the version of carbohydrate that works with your hormones rather than against them.

  • No gluten, no lactose, no lentils — remove completely
  • No paprika or bell peppers, no carrots, no white potatoes
  • Sweet potato: permitted and encouraged — slow-release, anti-inflammatory, high in potassium
  • No added sugar in any form, including honey, maple syrup, and agave
  • Anti-inflammatory focus: oily fish, leafy greens, olive oil, turmeric, ginger

Magnesium citrate supplementation is well-evidenced for insulin sensitivity in PCOS — continue. Vitamin D and Omega-3 are worth testing and considering with your GP; deficiency is common and the effect on muscle function and inflammation is meaningful.

04 — Recovery
Recovery — where the adaptation actually happens

Training provides the stimulus. Recovery is where the adaptation happens. The two are not separable — one without the other produces fatigue, not progress.

Deload every 5th week. Reduce volume by 40–50% and maintain intensity. This is not optional rest — it is when the nervous system consolidates the adaptations the previous four weeks demanded. PCOS-related cortisol dysregulation means deloads matter more for you than for a client without this condition. Do not skip them.

Hydration: minimum 3.1 litres on rest days, 3.5–4.0 litres on training days. Your total body water was below the expected range for your body composition at baseline. Consistent hydration affects energy, performance, and how InBody readings interpret your progress — it is one of the faster-acting levers available.

Sleep quality was not captured in the initial assessment. PCOS is associated with sleep-disordered breathing and elevated cortisol — both of which impair recovery and fat loss regardless of how well the training and nutrition are managed. If sleep feels non-restorative, raise it with your GP.

  • Magnesium citrate — continue; evidenced for sleep quality and insulin sensitivity
  • Vitamin D testing recommended — deficiency impairs muscle function and is common in PCOS
  • Omega-3 supplementation — anti-inflammatory; consider after GP review
  • Review checkpoints: Week 4 (in-session), Week 8 (full rescan), Week 12 (comprehensive)

Progress is not linear. There will be weeks where the numbers don't move the way you expect — and weeks where they shift significantly. The InBody data will tell us whether composition is changing even when the scale doesn't. That is precisely why we track both.
