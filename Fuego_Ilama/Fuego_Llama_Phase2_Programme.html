Daily — Both Athletes

Mobility.

Every day · ~10 minutes · before any session or independently

Done daily, not just on training days. These exercises maintain the hip, knee, and ankle range of motion that trail terrain demands — and that 60 km of volcanic rock will test. The cossack squat at the end doubles as activation before Session 1.

Mobility 01
Lying Hip CARs
5 reps each directionBoth legsSlow
Lie on your side. Draw the largest possible circle with your knee. Full range — move through end-range in every direction. No compensating at the spine.
Mobility 02
Knee CARs
5 reps eachBoth legsFull circle
Standing or supported. Circle the knee through its full range — flex, circumduct, extend. Keep the hip still. This is knee joint health work, not a stretch.
Mobility 03
Poor Man's Leg Extensions
10 reps eachControlled
Seated on a bench edge. Extend the lower leg slowly until fully straight. Hold 1 second. Lower slowly. Targets terminal knee extension and VMO activation — the muscle that fails first on long descents.

Mobility 04
Foot & Ankle Mobility
2 minBoth ankles
Ankle circles (10 each direction) + banded ankle distraction (30 s each) + toe spreads. Volcanic rock requires active, proprioceptively alive feet. This is not optional.
Mobility 05 / Activation
Landmine Cossack Squat
8 reps each sideBodyweight or light load
Wide stance. Shift into one hip, keeping the other leg straight. The landmine gives balance support — use it to reach full depth. Hip adductor and lateral chain activation.
Session 1 — Both Athletes · Together

Lower Power.

Tuesday · ~65 minutes · Primary race session

This is the session that wins the race. Eccentric quad for 3,800 m of descent. Glute power for 3,800 m of ascent. Lateral stability for volcanic rock. Every exercise has a direct race transfer — this is not general fitness training.

Duration
~65 min
Primary movers
Glute max · Quad (eccentric) · Hamstring · Hip stabilisers
Race transfer
Ascent power + Descent control
Frequency
1× weekly · never before a long run
S1 · 01
Barbell Hip Thrust
4 × 8Rest 90 sRPE 7–8
Drive through heels. Full hip extension — squeeze at the top, hold 1 s. Rib cage down. Do not hyperextend the lumbar spine.
Primary glute engine for sustained uphill climbing. The most loaded hip extension pattern in this programme.
S1 · 02
Bulgarian Split Squat
4 × 6 each3 s eccentricRest 90 sRPE 8
Rear foot elevated. Vertical torso. Front foot far enough forward that the knee stays over the ankle. The 3-second descent is non-negotiable — this is where the race adaptation happens.
The single most race-specific strength exercise in this programme. Loads eccentric quad (descent) and hip extensor (ascent) simultaneously.
S1 · 03
Nordic Hamstring Curl
3 × 5Full eccentric · 4–5 s descentRest 2 min
Hips fully extended throughout. Lower as slowly as possible — catch with hands at the bottom. Pull back using hamstrings only, not by hip-hinging. Reps are low because quality is everything here.
Most effective exercise for hamstring injury prevention. Eccentric strength protects the posterior chain on sustained, technical descents.
Client 2 — C4–C5/syrinx: keep bracing effort controlled. Do not strain or Valsalva. If any neck symptoms: stop immediately.
S1 · 04
Sissy Squat with Box
3 × 103 s downRest 60 s
Heels on box or plate. Lean back from the ankle — not from the hips. Control the descent fully. The burn is the point: this is patellar tendon conditioning.
Builds patellar tendon resilience for repeated quad loading over 60 km. Own the range before adding load.
Client 2 — right foot: monitor discomfort. Reduce range of motion or switch to bilateral goblet squat if symptomatic.
S1 · 05
Depth Step-Down
3 × 8 each3 s loweringRest 60 s
Stand on a step edge. Lower the free foot toward the floor — controlled, 3 seconds. Don't touch. Drive back up through the working heel. This is eccentric quad training, not a cardio exercise.
Direct simulation of descent mechanics. The same demand as 3,800 m of technical downhill under fatigue.
S1 · 06
Weighted Step-Up
3 × 10 eachRest 60 sProgress to pack weight
Drive through the heel of the working leg. Do not push off the trailing foot. Torso upright as if carrying a pack. Begin with DBs — build to a loaded pack by week 12.
Direct uphill simulation. The most transferable cardio-strength crossover in the programme.
S1 · 07
Pistol Box Squat
3 × 6 eachRest 60 s
Sit back to the box under full tension — never collapse onto it. Drive through the entire foot on the way up. Build depth before adding load. Full balance and proprioceptive demand.
Single-leg ankle and hip stability. Technical volcanic terrain demands unilateral control at end-range positions this movement directly trains.
S1 · 08
Lateral Bounds / Skater Jumps
3 × 8 eachRest 60 s
Absorb the landing on one leg — soft knee, controlled hip hinge. Landing quality matters more than jump distance. Pause 1 s on landing before the next bound.
Lateral force production for trail direction changes. Reactive ankle stability for uneven rock surfaces.
S1 · 09
Copenhagen Plank
3 × 20–30 s eachRest 45 sProgress: add hip dip
Top ankle or shin on the bench. Body in a straight line — hip stays level. Do not let it sag. Progress by extending hold duration, then adding a controlled hip dip.
Lateral hip stability for sustained single-leg stance. Far more race-specific than a standard side plank — this loads the full adductor and lateral chain under body weight.

Upper Body + Free Weights.

Wednesday · ~60 minutes · Hourglass architecture + pack endurance

Two goals in one session: shoulder-to-waist V-taper for Client 2's hourglass objective, and upper back endurance for hours of pack carriage across volcanic terrain. Farmer's carries and kettlebell swings are not optional — they are race simulation. Pull-up track runs every session.

Duration
~60 min
Primary movers
Lats · Rear delt · Traps · Hip extensors
Race transfer
Pack carriage endurance · Hip explosion
Client 2 bonus
Pull-up progression · Shoulder width
Pull-up split this session: Client 2 follows her progressive track (see Pull-Up Track tab — current stage). Boyfriend: weighted pull-ups 4 × 3–5, adding 5–10 kg. Both perform pull-up work first, before any other upper body fatigue.

S2 · 01
Pull-Up Work
4 setsRest 2 min
Always first — before any other upper body work. Full range of motion: dead hang at the bottom, chin over bar at the top. No kipping.
Client 2 — see Pull-Up Track
Boyfriend — weighted, 3–5 reps
S2 · 02
Lat Pulldown
3 × 10Rest 90 sRPE 7–8
Pull to upper chest. Elbows drive down and back. Torso ~10° back — avoid excessive lay. Full stretch at the top. Scapular endurance is the training goal here, not ego load.
S2 · 03
Single-Arm DB Row
3 × 10 eachRest 90 s
Brace the core. Row the elbow to hip, not to shoulder. Let the weight stretch at the bottom — full range. Anti-rotation demand is intentional.
S2 · 04
DB Shoulder Press
3 × 10Rest 90 s
Elbows at 45°. Press to full extension. Stop 2 reps short of failure. Head neutral throughout — never jut the chin forward.
Client 2 — C4–C5 / syrinx: strict neutral head. Never to failure. If any cervical symptoms begin: stop this exercise immediately.
S2 · 05
Lateral Raise
3 × 12–152 s descentRest 60 s
Lead with the elbow. Slight internal rotation. 2-second controlled descent. Do not shrug the trap — the medial deltoid is the target. Light load, high quality.
Shoulder width is the primary mechanism of hourglass silhouette. This exercise, done consistently, creates the visual ratio.
S2 · 06
Face Pull
3 × 15Rest 60 s
Pull to face height. External rotation at end range. Elbows high throughout. This is a postural endurance exercise — do not go heavy. The rear deltoid and scapular retractors are the target.
Prevents postural collapse under a 6–8 kg pack at hour 10. Upper back endurance is a race finisher.
S2 · 07
Farmer's Carries
3 × 40 mRest 90 sProgress to pack weight
Tall posture. Shoulders packed — not elevated. Walk with intent and pace. Load through the hips and core, not the neck. Time each carry and aim to beat it.
The closest gym exercise to 10 hours of self-supported pack carriage. Non-negotiable in Phase 2. Progress to a loaded pack by week 10.
Client 2 — C4–C5 / syrinx: load must stay through the hip belt equivalent — no neck/trap loading. Use DBs rather than a barbell yoke.
S2 · 08
Kettlebell Swings
4 × 15Rest 60 s
Hip hinge — not a squat. Let the bell float at the top; don't muscle it up. Explosive hip snap. Breathe: inhale on backswing, sharp exhale at the top. Back flat throughout.
Explosive hip extension power — the same neuromuscular pattern as pushing up a steep ascent. Transfers directly to climbing efficiency.
S2 · 09
Wall Dead Bug
3 × 8 each3 s per repRest 45 s
Lower back flat against the wall the entire time. If it lifts — you've gone too far. Slow and deliberate. This is anti-extension core training, not a stretch.
Core stability for descents and technical single-leg terrain. No Valsalva — C4–C5 safe.

S3 · 01
Goblet Squat
3 × 10Rest 90 sFull depth
Heels elevated if depth is limited. Chest tall, elbows inside knees at the bottom. Sit into it. Drive up through the full foot — not just the heels.
Client 2 — C4–C5: this is the preferred squat variation throughout. No barbell back squat.
S3 · 02
Single-Leg Deficit Deadlift
3 × 8 eachRest 90 s
Stand on a plate or low step. The deficit increases range of motion through the ankle and foot — own the bottom position. Hips square throughout. This is harder than a standard SL-RDL and more race-specific.
The deficit adds ankle and foot stability demands identical to volcanic trail terrain. Directly prepares the foot-to-hip chain for technical rock.
S3 · 03
Lateral Step-Up
3 × 10 eachRest 60 s
Step laterally onto the bench. Drive through the working hip — not the knee. The lateral pattern targets gluteus medius, which is critical for trail running hip stability during single-leg stance.
S3 · 04
Pallof Press
3 × 12 each side2 s holdRest 45 s
Feet hip-width. Slight knee bend. Press out and hold 2 s. The goal is zero rotation — resist the cable completely. The harder this feels, the more you need it.
Anti-rotation stability for single-leg stance on uneven rock. The core pattern used every step on technical terrain.
S3 · 05
Landmine Cossack Squat
2 × 8 eachSession finisher
Loaded variation — use the landmine for balance. Reach full depth on the working side. The opposite leg stays straight. Hip adductor strength and lateral chain mobility in one movement.
Client 2 Only — Session 2 · First Exercise

Pull-Up Progression.

12-week track · 4 sets every Session 2 · Goal: first unassisted pull-up

A full pull-up is achievable in 12 weeks from any baseline with the correct eccentric-first progression. Move to the next stage only when the current stage is solid — do not rush. Stage 3 (Banded) is the likely entry point for Phase 2.

C4–C5 / syrinx note: Pull-ups load through the shoulder girdle — not the cervical spine axially. This is generally safe with the C4–C5 modification, provided the head stays neutral throughout and loading is progressive. If any cervical symptoms appear during pull-up work, cease immediately and seek physio review before continuing.

Phase 2 · Both Athletes

Cardio & Elevation.

June – July 2026 · Building to 2×8 km + 1×18 km by end of Phase 2

Running volume is the most important training variable for a 60 km ultra. Strength supports it — it does not replace it. The StairMaster and treadmill incline sessions are the primary solution for training vertical gain in flat Bremen. These are not optional add-ons. Track cumulative vertical metres weekly.

Elevation prep — Bremen
StairMaster Protocol

Set resistance to moderate. RPE 6–7 — you should be able to hold a short sentence. 30–40 min per session. Track cumulative vertical on the machine display.

StairMaster conversion: ~1,500 steps ≈ 150 m vertical. A 35-min session at moderate pace ≈ 250–300 m vertical. Two sessions per week = 500–600 m — on target.

From week 10: add a small pack (5 kg) to StairMaster sessions. From week 14: build to 8 kg. This is the closest Bremen offers to race-day carry simulation on incline.

  • Week 20: Madeira recce if possible (4 hr flight). Walk levadas, feel volcanic rock surface. Mental preparation is as valuable as physical.
